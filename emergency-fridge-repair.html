When Is a Fridge Problem an Emergency?

Not every fault needs a midnight call-out, but some can't wait. Treat it as an urgent fridge repair if:

The Fridge Is Warm

The inside is above 5°C and getting worse.

The Freezer Is Thawing

Ice cream is soft, or food is starting to defrost.

No Power or No Light

The fridge is silent and the display is dead.

Water Is Leaking

Onto the floor or near electrical outlets.

Burning Smell or Loud Noise

Buzzing or clicking from the back.

Compressor Cuts In and Out

But the fridge never gets cold.

Business Stock at Risk

Your stock, or your next food inspection, is at risk.

What To Do While You Wait for the Technician

The first few hours decide how much food you save. Here is what to do right now:

  1. Keep the doors closed. Every time you open the fridge or freezer, cold air escapes.
  2. Check the basics. Make sure the plug is in, the power point works and the circuit breaker hasn't tripped.
  3. Don't keep switching it on and off. Restarting repeatedly can damage the compressor.
  4. Move key items. Put medicines, baby milk, meat and dairy into an esky or a working fridge with ice.
  5. Group frozen food together. A packed freezer stays cold longer than a half-empty one.
  6. Use a thermometer. Fridges should sit at 5°C or below, and freezers at −15°C or colder.
  7. Switch off at the wall if water is leaking near the fridge's electrics, and don't touch wet wiring.
  8. Take photos of the fridge, the model label and any error codes. This speeds up your diagnosis.

Please don't pull the back off a fridge or touch refrigerant lines yourself. Refrigerant work legally needs an ARC-licensed technician, and electrical faults can be dangerous.

How Long Is Food Safe Without a Working Fridge?

As a general guide, a closed fridge keeps food cold for around four hours without power. A full freezer can stay frozen for about 48 hours, and a half-full one for about 24 hours, as long as the door stays shut. Food that has been above 5°C for more than four hours should usually be thrown out, especially meat, seafood, dairy, cooked leftovers and cut fruit. When in doubt, throw it out. For official advice, check the NSW Food Authority guidance on power cuts and emergencies.

Food businesses have stricter rules. Under the Food Standards Code, potentially hazardous food must be held at 5°C or below, so a failing cool room needs urgent attention.

Prevent the Next Emergency

A little care can avoid a 2am breakdown:

  • Clean the condenser coils once or twice a year
  • Check door seals with the paper test
  • Leave a gap around the fridge for airflow
  • Avoid overfilling shelves and blocking vents
  • Keep temperatures at 3–4°C in the fridge and −18°C in the freezer
  • Fix noises, leaks and warm spots early, before they become emergencies

For businesses, regular servicing every three to six months is the best insurance against an after-hours failure.
